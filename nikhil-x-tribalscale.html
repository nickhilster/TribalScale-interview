NIKHIL × TRIBALSCALE

Different paths. Similar questions: how do we redesign products and workflows around what people and AI can now do together?

WHY → WORKFLOW → EVIDENCE → OUTCOME
0101 · Thesis

Start with the outcome

The interesting AI question is rarely “where can we add AI?” It is “what should this workflow become now?”

  • That is the common thread I see between my work and TribalScale’s current AI-native direction.
  • Design begins before the interface: people, constraints, decisions, handoffs and the outcome the system exists to create.
Sheetal — Don’t Add AI. Redesign the Company.
0202 · Practice

Design the workflow

My path has moved through animation/VFX, frontline operations, production, UX/product and AI — but the recurring work is workflow design.

  • Find the friction.
  • Understand the humans and constraints around it.
  • Prototype the smallest useful intervention.
  • Put it in front of people.
  • Measure, learn and change direction when the evidence says to.
0303 · Evidence

Best Buy

Before AI, the lesson was already the same: redesigning a workflow can matter more than adding another tool.

  • A service process was taking roughly seven days.
  • I worked on changing the operating workflow around the bottleneck.
  • The cycle moved to under 48 hours.
  • The durable lesson: improve the system people work inside, not just the surface they touch.
0404 · Evidence

RBH → independent build

A consultancy environment exposed an internal AI opportunity; the later independent build tested whether the architecture could stand on its own.

  • The exploration began from a workflow need, not a model demo.
  • The implementation separated predictable system behaviour from generative behaviour.
  • Human approval, guardrails and failure handling were treated as product architecture.
  • The independent build is distinct from the work performed while at RBH.
0505 · Evidence

LTB Buddy

A tenant does not need an “AI lawyer.” They need help moving through a difficult process without surrendering authority.

  • Organize information → understand the situation → prepare → identify the next step.
  • AI assists navigation; consequential decisions remain with people.
  • Evidence, uncertainty, escalation and review are part of the experience design.
  • The product is in public beta so real use can challenge the assumptions.
0606 · Craft

RyFine

AI-native thinking still has to become a coherent human experience.

  • Interaction design translates system capability into understandable choices.
  • Prototyping makes assumptions visible early enough to change them.
  • The interface is one layer of the product — alongside workflow, behaviour, trust and feedback.
0707 · Convergence

People + agents + systems

The human role becomes more important as machine execution increases.

  • People define intent, context, judgment and accountability.
  • Agents can absorb execution where the workflow and guardrails are clear.
  • The design problem is the relationship between them — including when the machine should stop.
  • Human-in-the-loop is not only a safety fallback; it is a mechanism for catching intent drift.

0808 · Product discipline

Define “working”

A compelling demo is not the same thing as a dependable product.

  • Define success before turning the agent loose.
  • Evaluate behaviour before the user or client becomes the test suite.
  • Make important system behaviour explainable.
  • Design for iteration after launch, not merely the launch itself.
Heather — 88% Use It. 7% Scaled It.
0909 · Delivery

Build together

Product, design and engineering make better AI decisions when they work concurrently rather than throwing requirements over walls.

  • Architecture changes experience possibilities.
  • Experience decisions change technical requirements.
  • Real constraints should enter discovery, not appear at handoff.
  • For consulting, the client team should gain capability while the product gains capability.
